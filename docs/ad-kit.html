01

The one thing the ad has to land

If a viewer remembers a single sentence, it is this one. Everything else in the film is evidence for it.

Most tools tell you that you spoke too fast. FounderVoice tells you why it happened, what it cost you, and the exact drill that fixes it — measured against your own history, not an average.

The demo does the arguing. Show a real sixty-second recording, then the report naming a habit the speaker did not know they had, then click the timestamp so the viewer hears the moment. Proof beats adjectives, and this product's proof fits inside twenty seconds of screen recording.

02

Who to point this at

Segments defined by the moment, not the person — which is also what the ad platforms let you target on interest and job title.

AFounders raising now

Pre-seed to Series A. Demo day is dated, the deck is done, the delivery is not. Highest intent, shortest consideration window.

Trigger: a meeting on the calendar next week.

BTechnical founders in the pitching seat

Built the product, now has to sell it. Knows the material cold and still rushes it. Responds to measurement, distrusts vibes-based coaching.

Trigger: “you lost them in the first minute”.

CSolo founders running their own demos

Repeats the same explanation twenty times a week and has never once heard it back.

Trigger: a call that went quiet after the price.

DAnyone about to be recorded

Podcast guests, panel speakers, conference talks, press. Long-form answers where rambling costs them the edit.

Trigger: an invite with a date on it.

EOperators giving board updates and standups

Short status that has to land without filler. The everyday case that decides how someone is read internally.

Trigger: a promotion into the room where they now speak.

FJob seekers with an interview booked

Talking about your own work under pressure is the same skill as the pitch. Big volume, cheapest clicks, fastest activation.

Trigger: “tell me about yourself”.

Not the audience, and never implied to be. This is not an English tutor, an accent-reduction app or a language product. Targeting “learn English”, “ESL” or “fluency” pulls a user the product will not serve and breaks the positioning. Target the moment — investor pitch, demo day, interview, board update — every time.

06

The 3:30 walkthrough

The video that lives on the site, the Product Hunt page and the YouTube channel. One screen recording, ten chapters, markers in the description.

0:00 — Cold open: the problem, in one recording

Record yourself answering “what does your company do” with no preparation. Play four seconds of it. Say: “That is me, unprepared. Here is what the product does with it.” Do not explain the product first — earn the next three minutes with the recording.

0:20 — First check: sixty seconds, no signup

Open foundervoice.app cold, in a clean browser window. Show that there is no account step. Take the prompt, speak for a minute, hit stop. Say the allowance out loud while it uploads: ten recordings every 24 hours, free, no card.

0:50 — Reading the report properly

The centrepiece, and the longest chapter. Walk the top stats — pace, fillers, clarity, pause quality, presence, monotone — then scroll to the smart transcript and click a sentence so it seeks and plays. Then the root-cause finding: observation, cause, fix. Then export the PDF, so the viewer sees the report is portable.

1:25 — Labs: turning the finding into a drill

Click “Open lab” straight from the report, so the connection is visible rather than described. Read the three parts of the card aloud — speak this, how to speak it, what this means — record the line, and show that the review grades only that one skill. Say why: “Pitch variation is not a failure during a pause drill. It waits for its own lab.”

1:55 — Practice: the investor who does not let go

Pick the investor round. Answer one question by voice, badly on purpose. Let the follow-up land. Point out that the voice answer also produced a full report of its own.

2:20 — Record: the long version, and file upload

Mic picker, push-to-talk, then drag and drop an existing file up to 100 MB — the moment for anyone with a demo-day recording already sitting on their drive. Mention Live Coach as in-browser hints, and be explicit that the real analysis happens after you stop.

2:45 — Listen: real calls, not rehearsals

Start a listening session, show clips collecting, end it, then complete a lab to unlock the verdict. Name the limitation on camera: the verdict waits for a drill so it has something clean to compare your real speech against. Explaining that honestly is more persuasive than hiding it.

3:05 — Voice Memory, Library and Coach

Progress curves over 7, 30 and 60 days with a real drop in filler rate. Then Library filtered to Labs. Then Coach: pick a goal, add your own filler words, and show Fresh start — including that it makes you type DELETE. The deletion story is a trust story.

3:20 — Privacy and price

Free tier: ten recordings every 24 hours and two investor practice rounds, no account and no card. Pro lifts both caps and is arranged through the contact form. If you are showing the local build, this is where you say the audio, the model and the database stay on your machine.

3:30 — Close

“Record sixty seconds. Find out what you actually sound like.” URL card, hold four seconds, out.

07

Hooks to test, and where each cut goes

Swap only the first four seconds. Everything after 0:04 stays identical, so the test measures the hook and nothing else.

Eight openers

  • “You have heard your pitch. You have never seen it measured.”
  • “Eleven fillers in fifty-eight seconds.” Number first, no context. Highest scroll-stop in cold feeds.
  • “You lost them in the first minute.” Straight into the feedback nobody ever explains.
  • “Nobody tells a founder they talk too fast. They just pass.”
  • “Slow down is not coaching.” Argues with advice the viewer has already been given.
  • “Your deck is done. Your delivery is not.” Best for raising founders, weakest for everyone else.
  • “Play four seconds of yourself back.” Direct instruction; works with a face-to-camera open.
  • “A hundred and seventy-eight words a minute is not confidence. It is nerves.”

Cut per channel

  • YouTube pre-roll — the :30. Skip lands at 0:05, so the readout must be on screen by 0:04.
  • Reels, Shorts, TikTok — the :30 reframed 9:16, zoomed to the readout, captions burned in, nothing depending on sound.
  • X — the :60 with the first frame already showing the report; autoplay is muted, so the numbers carry it.
  • LinkedIn — the :60, hook D or F, aimed at operators and interviewers rather than founders.
  • Product Hunt and site hero — the 3:30 walkthrough with chapter markers.
  • Landing page loop — a silent twelve-second cut: waveform, readout, lab card. No voiceover, no end card.
08

Before you hit record

The capture checklist. Most of these are only fixable before the shoot, not in the edit.

  • Seed the account with at least three real sessions. Voice Memory, the streak and the progress curves are all empty on a fresh install, and an empty chart is the one shot that makes a product look unfinished.
  • Give those sessions honest numbers. Do the bad take first, so the improvement curve in the Progress shot is real. A curve you faked is a curve someone will ask about.
  • Capture at 1440p or better, then crop. Type in this UI is small; a 1080p capture upscaled for Shorts turns the readout to mush.
  • Use the same mic in every scene. The product compares takes against each other — switching mics mid-video changes the numbers on screen.
  • Hide the browser chrome and every other tab. Bookmarks bar off, notifications off, one window, one profile.
  • Let the analysis finish rather than cutting around it. Hold the pending state for a beat; the honest wait reads as real software.
  • Keep the real click and playback audio. The timestamp-click-and-play moment needs its own sound to land.
  • Shoot the end card last, once the URL, the price line and the free-tier wording are final everywhere else.
09

Say this. Never say that.

Straight from the brand guidelines. The right-hand column is not a style preference — those phrases put the product in a category it does not compete in and will not serve.

Say

  • “Founder-level communication” — the category we claim.
  • Name the moment: the investor pitch, the Q&A, the board update, the interview.
  • “Pace, fillers, pauses, clarity, pitch variation” — the five things actually measured.
  • “Ten recordings every 24 hours. No account, no card.”
  • “Estimate” wherever the product itself labels a number an estimate.
  • “Record sixty seconds.” Plain imperative, no exclamation mark.

Never say

  • Improve your English · speak better English · fluency — out of scope entirely.
  • Non-native speaker — say founders, executives, public speakers.
  • Fix your accent — the product never scores anyone on sounding native.
  • Communication skills, unqualified — qualify it with founder, or with the moment.
  • Revolutionary · game-changing · unlock · supercharge — state the actual result.
  • Powered by AI — name the concrete thing it does.
  • FounderVoice AI · Founder Voice · Foundervoice — one word, one spelling, in every caption and end card.

Tone direction for the voiceover. Specific, causal, honest, unhurried. Short sentences, no exclamation marks, no rising sell. The read is a calm executive tool — if the voice sounds excited, the ad is contradicting the product.

10

What the ad is asking for

One call to action everywhere, because the product's first step is also its whole argument.

“Record your first minute.” Not “sign up”, not “learn more” — what is being asked for is the thing that proves the product. Every end card points at foundervoice.app and the onboarding check behind it.

Tag every destination so the hook test is readable: one campaign per cut, one source per channel, the hook letter in the content field. The only conversion event worth optimising toward is a completed first recording — not a page view, and not a click.

Primary CTA

Record your first minute — free, no account, ten recordings a day.

Secondary CTA

For the reading audience and search traffic: the nineteen guides at /guides.

Upgrade path

Pro lifts the daily cap and the two-round practice limit, arranged through the contact form. Mention it only in the long walkthrough.
